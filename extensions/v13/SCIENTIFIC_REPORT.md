## Summary

The revised manuscript retains a single question: which additional observations distinguish acquired cellular history from a change in the composition of adhesive phenotypes? Its positive contribution combines exact mechanistic observational equivalence, a calibrated experimental discrimination design and quantitative limits imposed by missing outcomes. New calculations address the external audit's unresolved scientific and presentation problems. All values below derive from synthetic models or public derived microscopy counts; no new bacterial wet-lab experiments were performed.

## Why two-arm loss causes a detection cliff

For originally assigned binary attachment outcomes, define arm-specific attachment rates `p1`, `p0`, independent founder loss fractions `e1`, `e0`, and the sharp lower score `ell = S1/N1 - (S0 + N0 - n0)/N0`. Then

`E(ell) = (1-e1)*p1 - p0 - e0*(1-p0)`.

An expected positive lower score requires `e0 < ((1-e1)*p1 - p0)/(1-p0)`. Under logistic/exponential kinetics, independent draws of the specified synthetic block population give sham probability 0.09443, daughter pulse probability 0.15927 and mother pulse probability 0.19515. With 10% exposed-arm loss, the daughter threshold for sham loss is 5.40%, versus 8.97% for mothers. The daughter endpoint limits joint detection. Under the distinct complementary-log-log/Weibull law the daughter threshold is 6.89%.

In 1,600 independently generated studies per fine-grid condition, logistic/exponential joint detection using assumption-free bounds under equal uniform loss fell from 99.3% at zero attrition to 94.7% (1%), 78.3% (2%), 59.1% (3%), 40.3% (4%), 23.3% (5%), 2.4% (7.5%), and 0% (10%). The recovered-only test remained at 99.2% at 10% uniform loss. That recovered-only rate applies when the loss mechanism is independent of the underlying response. The independent kinetic comparator reached 46.6% bounded detection at 5% loss and 2.9% at 10% loss.

## Assumption-assisted sensitivity and its failure control

An additional fixed-probability population had sham attachment rates `p_H=0.10`, `p_L=0.03`. If each unobserved sham Bernoulli success probability is **known** to be at most `u=0.10` and the missing outcomes are conditionally independent, the unobserved sham successes are stochastically dominated by `Binomial(M0,u)`. Their high quantile can replace the deterministic worst-case success count `M0` in a sensitivity analysis. This controls the missing sham contribution probabilistically, under the independent calibration assumption, not by pretending that a binary outcome is bounded above by `0.10`.

In 2,800 illustrative studies with 10% uniform loss in both arms, joint detection was 0.0% using the sharp assumption-free bound, 34.3% using the calibrated binomial-tail sensitivity test and 95.1% using recovered-only inference. With 5% loss, the corresponding values were 0.0%, 70.5%, and 95.9%. When a narrower, invalid `u=0.03` was substituted despite some actual missing sham success probabilities equal to `0.10`, the nominal high-quantile coverage of missing sham successes declined to 91.5% at 10% loss. These calibrations are hypothetical and require independent experimental validation in any biological application. The study-level cluster test uses an approximate Student-t threshold; the tail condition is verified by Monte Carlo, and no universal finite-sample error guarantee is claimed.

Both holding-time groups have one deposited run, precluding biological uncertainty estimates for the washout curve.
